The Program

Data science for a better world

This STEM-designated program builds data science and AI skills and applies them to questions that matter for people, communities, businesses, and policy. Understand how people behave, interact, and navigate the world. Use data and AI to drive decisions and make a positive impact.

What you’ll study

  1. 01

    Social Science

    How to ask the right questions
    4 units (1 course)

    Learn how social scientists model human decisions and population change, drawing on demography, economics, geography, political science, public health, and sociology. You’ll learn to frame questions data can answer, measure what matters, and connect findings to decisions in business and policy.

  2. 02

    Data Science and AI

    Causal inference and statistics
    8 units (2 courses)

    Learn descriptive, quasi-experimental, and experimental methods that describe the world and separate cause from correlation. Build replicable workflows and use AI tools to extend your analysis in hands-on assignments. You’ll learn to interpret output and judge when a claim holds up.

  3. 03

    Professional Skills

    Coding, collaboration, and communication
    8 units (2 courses)

    Build hands-on skills in programming, machine learning, and AI-assisted coding; managing large-scale datasets; and visualizing statistical results. Practice team collaboration, project management, and professional presentations to non-technical audiences.

  4. 04

    Data Science in Action

    Career seminar · 10 units

    Each week, internationally renowned speakers from academia, industry, government, and the nonprofit sector show how data science and AI are used to solve real problems and shape policy. Meetings with speakers provide career-building tips and networking opportunities.

  5. 05

    Mentored Capstone Project

    Apply your skills · 6 units (9 months)

    Over nine months, tackle a real-world question from research, industry, or public policy. Starting from a problem statement, you’ll build and carry out a work plan with a small team of peers and a faculty mentor, write briefs for stakeholders or policymakers, and finish with an original analysis and presentation.

  6. 06

    Choose from 40+ UCLA Electives

    Electives · 12 units (3 courses)

    Take courses by leading faculty across campus to deepen your expertise and broaden your horizons, including courses in demography, economics, geography, political science, public health, sociology, and statistics and data science.

Program FAQs

  • Are classes in person?

    Yes. All MPDS courses are taught in person on the UCLA campus. The program does not offer online or hybrid courses.

  • How large will my classes be?

    The Fall 2027 cohort is expected to have about 25 to 35 students. Required first-year core courses will be about the size of the cohort. Elective sizes are set by the department and instructor offering each course, so electives may be larger or smaller.

  • Can I finish the program early?

    Yes. With the program’s approval and an approved capstone project, you can take 10 to 12 units a quarter and graduate in 16 months instead of 20. Students on the accelerated track complete the capstone in a team with other accelerated students.

  • Can I do the program part-time?

    MPDS is designed as a full-time, in-person program, and there is no formal part-time track. With program approval, domestic students may extend their studies to as many as nine quarters by taking fewer courses each term.

    The feasibility of a part-time schedule depends on being able to take the required courses when they are offered. Core courses and the capstone run once a year in a fixed sequence, so postponing a required course may mean waiting a full year to take it. Students who extend their studies should also expect higher total costs, because campus fees and health insurance are charged for each quarter enrolled.

    International students on F-1 visas generally must stay enrolled full-time.

    If you are thinking about a longer schedule, please talk with the MPDS office to see if it is feasible and seek program approval.

  • Is an internship required?

    No. A summer internship between the first and second years is optional. Program advisors help you arrange one with a research center, government agency, nonprofit, or company.

  • Is MPDS a STEM-designated program?

    Yes. MPDS is designated a STEM program. This means that international graduates may be eligible for up to 36 months of Optional Practical Training (OPT). See the federal STEM OPT guidance.
