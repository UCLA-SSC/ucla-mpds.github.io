Who should apply?

No data science experience is required, only curiosity about people and a desire to use evidence to inform decisions.

MPDS welcomes students from many backgrounds. Some come from economics, sociology, public health, geography, or policy and want to upgrade their data science skills. Others come from data science or statistics and want to deepen their knowledge of social science and human behavior to inform their use of data.

The program’s curriculum is adaptable and will challenge both types of students. Graduates will be prepared for careers in business, technology, policy, health, government, nonprofits, and research.

Admissions requirements

  • Bachelor’s degree with a cumulative GPA of 3.0 or higher, preferably in a social science, public health, public policy, data science, or statistics.
  • Statistics preparation: An upper-level statistics course is recommended, but not required. Professional experience with data may be considered, and first-year courses include review material. Students with strong statistics and data science backgrounds may test out of required courses and take higher-level courses.
  • Work experience is not required.
  • GRE: Optional.
  • English proficiency (unless exempt): TOEFL iBT 4.5 (87 for tests taken before Jan. 21, 2026) or IELTS Academic 7.0. See English proficiency details.

What to submit

  • Statement of purpose

    The questions you want to answer with data, the preparation you bring, and how MPDS fits your goals.

  • Personal history statement

    The experiences and perspectives that have shaped your path.

  • Two letters of recommendation

    From instructors or supervisors who know your analytical work.

  • Unofficial transcripts

    From every college or university you attended.

  • Résumé

    Including research, internships, and any work experience.

  • Writing or coding sample (optional)

    A paper, report, or analysis that shows how you work with evidence (optional link to code).

English proficiency and international applicants

Unless you qualify for the exemption below, you must submit TOEFL iBT or IELTS Academic scores. Minimum scores:

4.5

TOEFL iBT

For tests taken on or after Jan. 21, 2026, matching ETS’s updated scoring. Tests taken before that date need a score of 87.

7.0

IELTS Academic

Overall band score.

  • Exemption: You do not need TOEFL or IELTS scores if you hold a bachelor’s or higher degree from an accredited university in the United States or in a country where English is the sole language of instruction. This applies to all applicants, including U.S. citizens and permanent residents.
  • Sending scores: Use UCLA institution code 4837. For TOEFL, enter department code 99 (“not listed”); scores must come directly from ETS, and photocopies are not accepted. IELTS scores go to the department and the Division of Graduate Education. MyBest TOEFL scores are not accepted.
  • Accepted tests: TOEFL iBT, including the Home Edition, and IELTS Academic, including IELTS Online. Scores are valid for two years. IELTS Indicator and the TOEFL iBT Paper Edition are no longer offered.
  • STEM designation: MPDS is designated a STEM program, and graduates may be eligible for up to 36 months of Optional Practical Training (OPT). (federal STEM OPT guidance)
  • Internships: International students who take a summer internship for credit enroll in a one-unit course and must obtain Curricular Practical Training (CPT) in accordance with federal regulations.

Admissions FAQs

  • What preparation do I need if I didn’t study statistics or data science?

    No data science expertise is required. An upper-level statistics course is recommended, but not required, and professional experience with data may be considered in its place. First-year courses include review material, so students from economics, sociology, public health, geography, policy, and related fields can build the foundation they need.

  • Do I need to know how to code?

    No. The program teaches programming in Python, R, and SQL, along with AI tools, starting in the first year.

  • What types of classes will I take?

    You’ll take 15 courses over six quarters: courses in social science and population, statistics and data science (including causal inference and AI tools), two professional skills courses, the Data Science in Action career seminar, a mentored capstone project, and three electives chosen from more than 40 UCLA graduate courses. Students with strong statistics and data science backgrounds may test out of required courses and take higher-level ones.

  • Is work experience necessary for admission?

    No. Work experience is not required, though the admissions committee considers every part of your application, including any professional experience with data.

  • May I apply to more than one graduate program at UCLA?

    No. UCLA policy allows one major or degree per application, except for approved concurrent or articulated degree programs.

  • How do I take the GRE or TOEFL?

    The GRE and TOEFL are given by the Educational Testing Service (ETS). See ets.org for test dates, locations, and the at-home editions. The IELTS Academic is given by the IELTS partners; see ielts.org.

  • Do I have to take the TOEFL?

    Only if you do not hold a bachelor’s or higher degree from an accredited university in the United States or in a country where English is the sole language of instruction. See the English proficiency details.

  • What is the English as a Second Language (ESL) requirement?

    Admitted students take UCLA’s English as a Second Language Placement Examination (ESLPE) when they arrive, unless they scored at least 5 on the TOEFL iBT (100 for tests taken before Jan. 21, 2026) or 7.5 on IELTS Academic. Depending on the results, students may need to take up to two ESL courses. If required, MPDS covers the cost of these courses.

  • Can I submit a writing sample?

    Yes, but it is not required. The admissions committee would be happy to consider a writing sample, such as a paper, report, or analysis, that you feel shows how you work with evidence.

  • What is the minimum GPA required for admission?

    A cumulative GPA of 3.0 on a 4.0 scale, or the equivalent, is required.

  • Is the GRE required? Is there a minimum score?

    No. GRE scores are optional, and there is no minimum score. However, applications that include GRE scores may receive preferential treatment. GRE scores are valid if taken within five years of the intended term of admission. If you submit GRE scores, the admissions committee considers them with the rest of your application.

  • Can I apply if I am still enrolled in an undergraduate program?

    Yes. Many applicants apply in their final year of college. Upload your transcripts to date and describe the courses you will take in your final year. You must finish your bachelor’s degree before classes begin in fall 2027.

  • What is the deadline to turn in all application materials?

    The final deadline for fall 2027 is June 30, 2027. Apply by the priority deadline of January 15, 2027, to receive a decision by March 1. Applications received after January 15 will be reviewed on a rolling basis.

  • Is there an application fee?

    Yes. The fee is $135 for U.S. citizens and permanent residents and $155 for all other applicants. It covers the cost of processing applications and is not refundable for any reason, including the outcome, the date you apply, when your application is reviewed, or if you withdraw it.

  • What transcripts are required?

    Upload transcripts from every college or university you attended. If you transferred, include the transcripts from each institution, even if your later school lists those courses.

  • Do I need to mail my official transcripts during the application process?

    No. Upload unofficial transcripts with your application. Official transcripts are not needed during the initial stages of the application process. You will be contacted if official documents are needed.

  • Will I need to submit an official transcript?

    Only if the program contacts you. If so, have the registrar of each college or university you attended send one official copy of your transcripts (and degree certificates, if applicable) directly to the program, or request official copies and mail them yourself in the sealed envelope provided by the issuing institution. Send them to:

  • How should I submit my letters of recommendation?

    Your recommenders upload their letters directly to the online application. Letters sent by mail or email are accepted only with permission from the MPDS office.

  • Can I submit my application before my recommenders have sent their letters?

    Yes. Letters are added to your application as they arrive.

  • Can I update my application after the deadline?

    No. Application materials cannot be changed after the deadline.

  • Can I talk with someone before I apply?

    Yes. Join a live Zoom info session with the faculty, or write to us at mpds@ccpr.ucla.edu.
