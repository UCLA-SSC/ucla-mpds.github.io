Tuition and fees

Tuition is $1,350 per unit, or $32,400 a year, at the same rate for California residents and non-residents. The full 20-month program comes to $64,800 in tuition. Students also pay UCLA campus fees of just under $2,000 a year and student health insurance of about $7,500 a year, which can be waived with comparable coverage. Together, the estimated total is about $84,000, excluding materials, housing, and personal expenses.

Ways to pay for your degree

  1. 01

    Merit fellowships

    MPDS expects to award substantial merit fellowships using the Cota-Robles criteria. Every applicant is considered automatically. The program will contact recipients.

  2. 02

    Financial aid

    UCLA offers a variety of financial aid resources and tools for students. Visit the UCLA Division of Graduate Education financial aid page for details.

    Employer support

    Many employers help pay for graduate study. If yours needs documents for internal approval, such as a program description, cost estimate, or enrollment letter, write to us.

    Veterans should confirm their education benefits with UCLA’s School Certifying Official. See the Veteran Resource Center’s Chapter 33 GI Bill page for details.

Cost and financial aid FAQs

  • Is tuition the same for California residents and non-residents?

    Yes. The same per-unit rate applies to all MPDS students.

  • Are fellowships available?

    Yes. MPDS expects to offer substantial merit-based fellowships. All applicants are considered automatically; no separate application is needed, and recipients are contacted by the program. Fellowships are awarded using the criteria of UCLA’s Eugene Cota-Robles Award, which recognizes strong academic potential and a sustained commitment to education, research, and public service that benefits communities. Because MPDS is a professional degree, we also recognize a commitment to using data to benefit communities through careers in business, government, and the nonprofit sector.

  • What borrowing options are available?

    Visit UCLA Financial Aid & Scholarships. U.S. citizens or permanent residents may also wish to visit the federal FAFSA website. UCLA’s Title IV School Code is 001315. Parental information is not required for graduate students.

  • How does a Federal Direct Unsubsidized Loan work?

    U.S. citizens or permanent residents may apply for a Federal Direct Unsubsidized Loan by filing the FAFSA. The annual loan limit is $20,500, at a fixed interest rate of 8.07% (2026–27) with a 1.057% origination fee. Repayment begins six months after the student graduates or drops below half-time enrollment. For loans first disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, borrowers choose between a tiered standard plan (10 to 25 years, depending on the amount borrowed) and the income-based Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP). A credit check is not required, and there is no prepayment penalty. Find more information at StudentAid.gov.

  • Can I waive student health insurance?

    Yes, if you have comparable coverage. You can request a waiver of the UC Student Health Insurance Plan (UCSHIP) each year (see UCLA’s UCSHIP waiver instructions).

  • Is the program eligible for the Post-9/11 GI Bill?

    Yes. Find the details on the UCLA Veteran Resource Center’s Chapter 33 GI Bill page.

  • What is not included in the cost estimates?

    Materials (e.g., computer, software, books), housing, and personal expenses.

  • Will tuition and fees change?

    Tuition, campus fees, and student health insurance are set each year by the University of California. The figures on this page are estimates for students entering in fall 2027.
